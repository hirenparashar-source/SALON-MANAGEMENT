No. 06

The House

A short history, and the rules we keep. Both are older than this website.

06

Meher Kapur opened the first chair in 1987 above a stationer on Fountain Lane, with one mirror, one hairdryer and a card in the window that said Cutting, by appointment. The card is still in the drawer. The mirror is still on the wall.

What changed is the discipline. Colour became a chemistry we study rather than a product we buy; cutting became a consultation before it became a scissors; and the book — the diary of who sat where and when — became something we could actually read back.

A haircut should look like the person, only more awake.

We are seven chairs now, a skin room, a nail table and a bridal chambre that takes four hours and one photograph. We close on Monday because a salon that never closes eventually stops caring how it looks.

Everything on this page is run from one small program in the back office: the diary, the till, the shelf, the receipts and the reminders. It keeps its own audit trail, and it will hand you a copy of your record — or erase it — whenever you ask.

House rules

Your record

Creating an account is optional. You can book, pay and keep a receipt without one; an account simply lets the diary remember you across devices. Ask the desk for an export or an erasure at any time — it takes one screen and one signature in the audit log.
